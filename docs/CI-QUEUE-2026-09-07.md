# CI queue, measured: build.yml over 24 hours (2026-09-06T05:05Z – 2026-09-07T04:46Z)

Asked by lego-ac on 2026-09-07 after landings queued for up to 40 minutes behind the account's
runners. Everything below is read from the GitHub Actions API (`/actions/runs` and
`/actions/runs/{id}/jobs`) for this repo and the two sibling repos that share the account's
runners (sb3-creator, bw-board; bw-circuit-ui and emu8051-stc had no runs in the window). The
scripts that produced it are not committed; the method is stated so the numbers can be redone.

**Definitions.** *Queue wait* of a run = its first job's `started_at` minus the run's
`created_at`. *Running jobs at a moment* = jobs whose `started_at ≤ moment < completed_at`,
across all three repos. Jobs that wait on `needs:` (deploy, verify-gui) are excluded from every
job-level wait figure, because the API reports them as queued while they are merely waiting for
their predecessors. Runner-minutes are `completed_at − started_at` summed over jobs.

The Free plan's ceiling for a public repository is 20 concurrent Linux jobs. The account peaked
at 16, and that peak coincided with no waiting at all. **While runs waited, the account was
running a mean of 2.4 jobs, and for a third of all waiting time it was running nothing.** The
wait was not our concurrency. It was GitHub's queue: runners not being handed out, in two
windows.

way out of on standard runners.

## 2. Superseded runs that were not cancelled

build.yml's `concurrency` group is per commit (`pages-<sha>`), so a newer push or dispatch
on the same branch never cancels an older one. A run is *superseded* here when a newer run on
its branch was created before it finished.

| | main | other branches |
|---|---|---|
| superseded runs that ran to completion | 27 | 1 |
| runner-minutes spent after supersession | 280.8 | 0.5 |
| runs cancelled AFTER being superseded (by hand) | 6 | 16 |

Runner-minutes spent on superseded work: **281.3 of 3169.8, 8.9 %**.
Share of job queue-wait time during which the runners in use belonged to a superseded run:
**10.4 %** (sampled every 30 s over every first-wave job wait longer than a minute). The other
~90 % of the waiting happened behind nothing of ours.

Main's per-commit group is deliberate (LANES: "main gets one group per commit and is never
cancelled") — each main push wants its own verdict, and since 2026-09-06 a prose-only push
starts no run at all. The superseded minutes on main are the price of that verdict.

## 3. What each lever buys, with the numbers

| lever | buys | costs | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `concurrency` group per **branch** that cancels superseded dispatches (main untouched) | the 16 hand-cancels a day the fleet already performs (mean 1.7 min after supersession) and the 36 runner-min those runs burned before someone got to them; plus the single superseded branch run that ran to completion (0.5 min) | nothing — a superseded dispatch on a branch is a verdict on a commit nobody will merge as-is | **do it, for the hand-cancels**, not for minutes: ~36 runner-min a day on a public repo where runner-minutes are free, and no measurable share of the wait |
| the same group on **main** | 281 runner-min a day (27 superseded main runs), and at most a tenth of the wait — the share of waiting time spent behind our own superseded runs was 10.4 %, nearly all of it main | every main push loses its own verdict when the next push lands within ~9 min; on 2026-09-06 that was 27 of 65 pushes — the fleet would no longer learn WHICH commit broke main | **owner's call**; LANES chose the per-commit verdict deliberately, and prose-only pushes already start no run |
| fewer jobs per run (one browser job again, or legs downloading the build job's artifact) | ~270 s of duplicated setup per run × 142 runs ≈ 639 runner-min/day | the verdict gets SLOWER: the shards exist to run the two legs beside the build job, and a leg that waits for the build job's artifact is back on the serial path (+~5 min a run) | **no** — it trades the one thing measured to matter (time to verdict, 15.3 → 9.0 min) for minutes that are free |
| paid runner tier | Team raises the concurrency ceiling to 60; larger runners shorten job time | money, monthly | **no** — the ceiling was never reached (peak 16 of 20, with zero wait at that peak), and no tier shortens GitHub's allocation queue on standard runners |

**Recommendation to the owner.** Add a per-branch cancelling `concurrency` group for
non-main refs (one workflow expression; main keeps its per-commit group) — it automates the 16
cancels a day the fleet does by hand and costs nothing — and otherwise accept the queue: the
20-minute waits were GitHub allocating runners slowly in two windows, with the account running
0–3 jobs, and none of the three levers reaches that. What the fleet can do in such a window is
not stack dispatches into it: a run created at 23:51 waited 22 minutes behind a run created at
23:49 that itself waited 24, on an account with nothing running. The cheapest fix to the
*experience* is already in place — the shard split took the run's own critical path from 15.3
to 9.0 min, more than the median and p90 waits combined. If the owner wants main's 281
superseded minutes back, that is a policy change (which commit broke main), not a CI one.
