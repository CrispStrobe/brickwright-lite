ORBITAL LOOM -- TWO-MOTOR TECHNIC DRAWING MACHINE
Version 1.0 | 20 October 2026
===============================================

WHAT IT DOES

A geared turntable rotates the paper while an overhead motor swings a pen
around a second, offset axis. Their coordinated motion draws closed geometric
rosettes. Changing the rotation ratio produces five-, seven-, eleven-, or
eightfold patterns without rebuilding the mechanism.

This is an original digital prototype, not a physically built or tested model.
The rendered ink is a mathematical prediction, not a photographed drawing.

HARDWARE AND SUPPLIES

The supplied parts_list.csv is authoritative: 210 LEGO elements/assemblies,
21 distinct part shapes, including one SPIKE Prime hub 45601 with battery and
two medium angular motors 45603. No sensors or third motor are required.
LDraw identifies these electronics as 45601c01.dat and 54696p01.dat.
This is a parts-based design, not an alternate build from one particular set.

Most structure is Technic. The writing surface uses a 16 x 16 System plate
and two 8 x 16 tiles. Approximate assembled CAD envelope: 247 x 208 x 232 mm
(width x depth x height), excluding cable slack. The tiled platter is 128 mm
square. Part availability in every pictured color has not been verified;
substitute available colors while keeping the same part geometry.

Also provide a 120 mm paper disk, a light marker no longer than 120 mm and
about 8 mm in diameter, two elastic bands, and small pieces of masking tape.
Paper, marker, bands, and tape are not included in the LEGO inventory.

ASSEMBLY ORDER AND DETAILS THAT MATTER

1. Bearing base: build 01_base.ldr with the two vertical 5 x 11 frames,
   lower cross rails, central splices, and turntable bottom. The turntable
   attaches through four horizontal pins at z = +/-20, not its center holes.

2. Table drive: fit motor A and its mounting rails. The pinion is a 20-tooth
   double-bevel gear driving the 60-tooth turntable. In LDraw coordinates,
   the turntable origin is y=-70; the pinion center is (-100,-60,0).
   That half-stud vertical offset is intentional. Use the 4L axle centered
   at y=-50 and both half bushes, centered at y=-45 and y=-75. Do not raise
   the pinion to the ring origin or omit an axle retainer.

3. Gantry: build the four overlapping columns, upper rails, feet, and eight
   diagonal braces. The diagonals use exact 6-8-10-stud triangles. Fit the
   overlap pins and splice pieces before closing access with later modules.

4. Pen motor: mount B with its output facing down and its body/cable extending
   toward +X. Motor A faces up. Keep the overhead assembly rigid.

5. Hub: the hub center is at z=220. Its four mount positions include two
   shared 3L pins through the lower rail/splice and two upper pins. Follow
   the model's long-pin placement instead of adding duplicate short pins.

6. Platter: four perpendicular pin-hole connectors 15100 support the plate
   through four stud half-pins 4274. Point the integral connector pins inward
   into the turntable, with the half-pin studs upward. These mounts clear the
   turntable rim and spindle. The plate and tiles are intentionally shifted
   +10 LDU in both local X and Z -- half a stud -- to align their stud grid.
   Keep the modeled gear phase; center the paper on the turntable axis,
   not the shifted plate.

7. Pen holder: assemble the red crank, perpendicular-hole biscuit, and 9L
   marker rail. The assembly's 90-degree orientation matches B's rotor holes.
   Secure the marker against the rail with two bands and adjust its height
   for light contact. The modeled parked crank phase is -45 degrees.

8. Paper and leads: tape the centered paper down outside the drawing area.
   Connect A to hub port A and B to port B. Cables are omitted from the CAD.
   B's standard lead is 250 mm: lead it outside the motor's outer end, then
   along a loose diagonal back to port B. Do not consume its reach by routing
   tightly down a pillar. Keep both leads clear of the crank and platter.

FIRST RUN

With power off or motors coasting, lift the pen and turn both axes through
complete revolutions by hand. Confirm free rotation, sound gear engagement,
and cable clearance throughout. Adjust the marker before applying power;
the machine has no automatic pen lift or pressure control.

The optional Python program requires Pybricks firmware. It does not run in
the stock LEGO SPIKE app, and nothing in this package installs firmware.
Follow orbit_controller_notes.txt for the small pen-up direction checks.

In drawing mode, LEFT selects a preset; RIGHT records the current manually
positioned pose as zero and starts after one second. CENTER remains the
normal stop button. Both axes hold at completion; lift the pen before
releasing them for the next setup. There is no hard-stop homing.

Preset       Crank/table ratio    Table turns    Drawing motion
Fivefold           5/1                 1            22 seconds
Sevenfold          7/2                 2            42 seconds
Elevenfold        11/4                 4            82 seconds
Eightfold          8/3                 3            62 seconds

The program uses a 3 rpm table cruise and shared two-second speed ramps.
For fivefold motion, motor references reach A=-54 and B=+90 degrees/second,
after accounting for their opposite-facing output shafts.

Without Pybricks, the control concept can be reproduced in your preferred
LEGO app: run the paper clockwise at 3 rpm and the crank clockwise at 15 rpm
for the fivefold pattern. The motor shaft speed magnitudes have ratio 3:5.
Use encoder-based speed control if available; equal power percentages are
not a reliable ratio. Independently timed motor runs can demonstrate the
pattern, but do not provide the supplied controller's coordinated closure.

GEOMETRY, VALIDATION, AND LICENSE

The crank axis is 32 mm from the paper axis. The pen radius is 22.627 mm,
giving a maximum ideal trace radius of 54.627 mm on the 60 mm-radius paper.
The JSON gives the equations, including the -45-degree parked phase.

Digital checks cover file syntax, part references, rigid transforms, reviewed
mounting geometry, and selected motion clearances. The controller was parsed
and its reference trajectories checked numerically. Physical stiffness,
friction, cable routing, paper slip, and drawing quality remain untested.
